# Style guide: plain-English rewrite

Goal: same facts, much easier to read. This is a certification book, so facts must not change.

## Never change
- Every number, default, limit, price, version and date.
- Every `code term`, parameter, endpoint, header and model name, spelled exactly.
- Every citation marker like `<sup>[1]</sup>`, kept next to the claim it supports.
- Tables: keep every row and column, simplify the wording only.
- Chapter number, title and section order.

## Structure of each section
1. **The idea first.** One or two sentences saying what this section is about, before any detail. Define any term the first time it appears.
2. **One analogy per chapter**, woven through the text. Pick it in the first section and reuse it. Every time the analogy mentions a thing, name the real term right next to it in bold, like "the context window (the desk)". The exam uses the real words, never the analogy.
3. **Short labelled parts** that you can scan, such as "What's on the desk?", "The problem", "What to do". Bullets for lists.
4. **ELI5 at the end of each section.** Two to four sentences, a child could follow.

## Language
- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Everyday words: "use", "fix", "so". No "leverage", "utilize", "robust", "seamless", "comprehensive", "ensure", "enhance", "streamline".
- Say "because" next to the thing it explains.
- Concrete examples over abstractions.
- Explain jargon in a few words in brackets the first time, like "tokens (small chunks of text)".
- Bold only real terms the exam uses. No bold scatter for emphasis.
- No em-dash chains. No padding like "it should be noted".
- Do not add facts that are not in the original. If the original is unclear, keep its claim and say it plainly.
